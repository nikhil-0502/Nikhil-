Research Interests
Stochastic Modelling Derivative Pricing Credit Risk Market Microstructure Welfare Economics

My name is Nikhil Rajan. I recently completed my undergraduate degree in Mathematical Economics at the Madras School of Economics and am now persuing my postgraduate degree in quant finance at the London School of Economics. What drew me in was seeing how ideas from measure theory, especially risk-neutral expectations, are used directly in option pricing models like Black Scholes.

Since then, I have been building and testing models around that: simulating GBM paths, pricing options with Monte Carlo, and checking how sensitive outputs are to discretisation, volatility, and parameter shifts. Most of this I have implemented in Python and C++, with particular interest in how pricing errors, convergence, and simulation variance behave under different modelling choices.

I am most interested in how model outputs change when you vary things like the time step, volatility assumptions, or the numerical scheme being used. My current projects span market microstructure (liquidity regimes as Markov chains), credit risk (reconstructing the Jarrow-Lando-Turnbull framework from first principles, and a hidden Markov model for credit stress detection), financial contagion between the S&P 500 and Nifty 50, and a dynamic welfare analysis of sin taxation using Markov consumption models calibrated to NSSO data.

At Madras School of Economics, I have held 7 teaching assistantships covering stochastic processes, game theory, time series analysis, microeconomics, multivariable calculus, and finance. I maintained a CGPA of 9.61 and was awarded the University Gold Medal for graduating Rank 1 in my batch. I have also been on the Dean's List in every graded semester.

Outside academics, I am a huge football fan, especially of Real Madrid CF, and closely follow both La Liga and the Premier League. For fun, I sometimes build small prediction models around football using recent form, player ratings, xG trends, injuries, and fixture difficulty to estimate title races and relegation probabilities over a season. I also follow cricket across all formats and end up watching almost every major sporting event at some point.

Implied volatility, the volatility smile, and an introduction to local and stochastic volatility models.
